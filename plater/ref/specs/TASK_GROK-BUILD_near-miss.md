## Claude's method (running now on the laptop; numbers follow in a REPORT CLAUDE-AGENT - membrane ... file)
Film = membrane, uniform tension T, pinned on the span rectangle. On a layer, cells still stuck to the film are held at unit lift (w = 1); the free film satisfies Laplace's equation. The film slope at the edge of the bonded set is the peel angle per mm of lift; T x slope is the line load. Peel advances where the slope is largest; re-solve; repeat. Per piece: total film force F = T x (slope summed around its bonded edge), per mm of lift, and F / raft area.

## Claude's prediction from that physics, BEFORE the numbers (so you can shoot at the reasoning)
1. The edge-to-centre band falls out of the membrane by itself: rim pieces are closest to the pinned frame, so the film is steepest there and they peel first; pieces inside are shielded because the film between close neighbours stays nearly flat at lift height.
2. A piece finishing inside the ring is unloaded from one side (an arc). The piece that is LAST, alone, holds a tent of film from all sides at once (closed loop).
3. For one isolated round raft of radius a in a film of half-span R, w = ln(R/r)/ln(R/a), so the total film force is F = 2 pi T / ln(R/a) per unit lift: almost the SAME newtons whether the raft is small or large. So force per raft area goes roughly as 1/a^2. With T = 1056 N/m and R about 105 mm: a 519 mm2 raft (a = 12.9 mm) takes about 3.2 N and about 6 kPa per mm of lift; a 3955 mm2 raft (a = 35.5 mm) about 6.1 N and about 1.5 kPa per mm of lift.
4. So the same film, same tension, same lift puts about four times the stress on a small raft that is left holding the tent near the end of the ring as on a big one. The film side lets go a line at a time; the build-plate side of that small raft has to carry the whole force. Expected signature: small pieces near where the ring ends come off the build plate or off their supports while everything around printed clean.
